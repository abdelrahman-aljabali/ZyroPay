A direct x402 payment pays the seller before you know what you will get. With escrow, the money goes
to Masumi's payment-v2 `vested_pay` script (Plutus V3) instead. The seller collects it only after
recording a result hash on chain and waiting until an unlock time; until then you can ask for a
refund. x402 carries this as `assetTransferMethod: "masumi"`: the 402's `payTo` is the escrow
script, and the seller signs the terms.

ZyroPay only ever locks into the **canonical** deployment:
`addr_test1wzs4e6wc95hkwezlccjw9mdvq0r0rsgx6zk34avptga3ftgn37w4g` on preprod, with 2-of-3 admins
for disputes and a 7-minute cooldown between actions.

## Choosing escrow

`pay_x402` takes `escrow`:

- `true`: pay into escrow (the 402 must offer the `masumi` method);
- `false`: pay directly;
- omitted: escrow when the 402 offers it **and** the agent's policy sets an escrow threshold
  (`x402EscrowAboveUsdMinor`), otherwise direct.

With a threshold set, a direct x402 payment valued above it, or with no valuation, is treated like an
over-limit request (`ESCROW_REQUIRED`): it comes to you, or is denied if you chose that. A threshold
of 0 means every x402 payment must use escrow.

## From quote to lock

<Diagram
  name="escrow-flow"
  alt="Sequence diagram of an escrow payment, steps 1 to 15, as described in the list below."
  caption="An x402 payment into Masumi escrow. Numbers match the list below."
/>

1. The agent calls `pay_x402` with `escrow: true`.
2. The MCP worker requests the seller's escrow route.
3. The seller answers `402`: `payTo` is the escrow script, `extra` carries the terms signed by the
   seller's key (CIP-8 / COSE over the terms digest), the input commitment and the deadlines.
4. The worker passes the requirements to the API.
5. The API verifies, with `@x402/cardano` 2.29's own functions plus its own rules: the seller's
   signature over the terms; the recomputed input hash; `payTo` equal to the derived canonical escrow
   address; the deadline order and a 30-day horizon; `payByTime` at least 60 s away and within
   `maxTimeoutSeconds`; a commitment that names the paid URL; and, if the seller claims a registry
   identity, that identity [on chain](/how-it-works/discover/). Any failure refuses the payment.
6. It builds the datum and the exact locked lovelace (with live `coinsPerUtxoByte`), round-trips the
   datum field by field, and puts the terms and the **exact datum bytes** into the payload, so your
   approval commits to them.
7. You approve with your passkey.
8. The agent's next `get_approval_status` call picks up the signed lock.
9. The MCP worker retries the URL with it in `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`.
10. The seller's facilitator checks the lock (one inline-datum output to the escrow, exact value,
    datum equal to the signed terms, validity ending by `payByTime`) and submits it.
11. The seller answers `200` with the report, `X-Escrow-Result-Hash` and a delivery receipt.
12. The seller records `result_hash = SHA-256(body)` on chain with **SubmitResult**.
13. The MCP worker reports what it received: the receipt and its own SHA-256 of the body.
14. The API reads the escrow's datum from the chain.
15. It compares the two hashes: that is [proof before release](/how-it-works/proof/).

The lock transaction has one output to the escrow with the inline datum and exactly the locked value,
plus change to you. Its validity ends at `payByTime`, rounded down to a slot, and the request itself
expires at `payByTime`. No script runs at lock time, so the lock's rules are checked off chain, by
ZyroPay's verifier and by the facilitator's.

## The escrow's life

  alt="Escrow state chart: from FundsLocked, the seller's SubmitResult leads to ResultSubmitted and, after unlock_time, the seller's Withdraw; the buyer's SetRefundRequested leads to RefundRequested, or to Disputed after a result; the seller's AuthorizeRefund leads to RefundAuthorized and the buyer's WithdrawRefund; after submit_result_time without a result the buyer can withdraw a refund directly; a dispute can be settled by 2 of 3 admins after the dispute time."

| Deadline (from the seller's 402) | Demo seller default         | After it                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `payByTime`                      | 402 time + 5 min            | The lock can no longer land                                  |
| `submitResultTime`               | `payByTime` + 10 min        | Without a result, you can withdraw your refund on your own   |

| `externalDisputeUnlockTime`      | `unlockTime` + 15 min       | The admins (2 of 3) can settle a dispute                     |

## Getting a refund

In the app, an escrow payment shows its state from the chain, the deadlines and the refund action
that is open to you. Both buyer actions are script spends signed with your passkey:

- **Request refund** (SetRefundRequested). Possible while the funds are locked. Before a result it
  moves to `RefundRequested`; after a result it moves to `Disputed`. The demo seller's sweeper
  authorizes refund requests automatically (`AUTO_AUTHORIZE_REFUNDS`, its preprod demo policy).
- **Withdraw refund** (WithdrawRefund). Possible once the seller has authorized the refund, or after
  `submitResultTime` if no result was ever submitted. After a result, a refund needs the seller's
  authorization or the admins.

For each, the API builds the spend, has Blockfrost evaluate the script and adds a 20 % margin to the
measured units, sets the continuing datum (your cooldown is the validity end plus 420 s), and returns
a review whose payload is the spend's body hash. On submit it re-verifies the spend against fresh
protocol parameters, re-reads the escrow output, checks the stamp like an approval, claims the build
by compare-and-set (one signature per build), then attaches the witness and submits. A refund spend
needs a pure-ADA output in your wallet for Plutus collateral.

## How the seller gets paid

Right after answering, the seller's worker waits up to about 20 s for the lock, builds SubmitResult
with the Evolution SDK, has Blockfrost evaluate it, signs with its key and submits. A cron sweeper
runs every minute: it retries a pending SubmitResult, withdraws once chain time passes `unlockTime`,
and authorizes refund requests. The seller pays the fees of its spends (about 0.7 ADA each, with the
10 KB script inline) and needs its own collateral. Before it pays a fee for any output at the escrow
address, it checks that the datum names it as seller, that at least 4 tADA is locked and that there is
no reference script, because anyone can create an output at a script address.

Why at least 4 tADA: the escrow output must still clear the minimum UTxO value once the result hash is
in the datum (about 3.7 tADA). At 4 tADA or more the buyer's collateral return is 0 and the lock is
exactly the price. The demo seller charges 5 tADA on its escrow route.
